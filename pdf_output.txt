THE PROBLEM AND ITS BACKGROUND 
 
1.1 Project Context 
The floral industry, particularly within the events sector, operates in a fast-paced 
and highly demanding environment where precision and timeliness are essential. Staff 
are required to produce flower arrangements for occasions such as weddings, 
corporate functions, and celebrations, all within strict deadlines. The perishable 
nature of fresh flowers compounds this pressure significantly, as cut flowers lose 
market value rapidly after harvest, necessitating constant stock replenishment to 
minimize waste (Maheshwari et al., 2021). 
Client preferences further strain operations, as requests for rare imported varieties or 
locally available flowers require advance planning that manual systems cannot 
reliably support. Without adequate planning tools, even minor oversights can result in 
wilted arrangements or budget overruns. Companies that rely on fragmented, 
paper-based workflows such as physical notebooks, wall calendars, and informal 
social media messaging risk falling behind more organized competitors. These 
manual approaches create critical vulnerabilities including scheduling conflicts, 
inaccurate material estimation, undetected stock shortages, and the inability to 
systematically interpret client-provided inspiration images, all of which result in 
recurring operational delays and financial leakage.  1

1.2 Background of the Study  
Raflora Enterprises has maintained a consistent presence in the local floral 
industry since 2013, a strength that grants the company a stable market reputation for 
weddings, debuts, and corporate functions. While the company typically operates 
with a core staff of 10 to 15, the ability to scale to 60 personnel during peak seasons 
is an operational requirement. However, this capacity is currently hampered by a 
manual operational framework. Relying on physical notebooks, wall calendars, and 
fragmented social media messaging creates critical weaknesses that negate the firm’s 
competitive advantage.

Planning, and Confirmation and Execution. In the initial phase, client preferences are

1.5 Scope and Limitation 
The study focuses on the design and development of a web-based Flower 
Arrangement Event Booking and Inventory Management System with Gemini 
AI-Assisted Image Analysis, specifically tailored for the operations of Raflora 
Enterprises. The system is designed to transition the business from a manual, 
paper-based workflow to a centralized digital ecosystem. The system is accessible via standard web browsers and implements role-based 
access control (RBAC) for three distinct user roles:  
Admin: Responsible for managing the overall booking calendar, validating 
AI-generated material suggestions, overseeing inventory levels, and generating 
operational reports. 
Client: Accesses a self-service portal to submit event booking requests, upload 
inspiration images for AI analysis, and receive price quotations. 
Staff: Utilizes the system to view assigned event checklists, update physical stock 
levels, and log the return of non-perishable materials. 
The functional core of the system is composed of the following modules: 
Event Booking Coordination: Manages the lifecycle of a reservation, from initial 
request and scheduling to final confirmation. It supports both pre-set packages (with 
automated material lists) and customized arrangements. 
Gemini AI-Assisted Image Analysis: Utilizes the Gemini AI API to analyze 
client-uploaded inspiration photos. The AI interprets visual data to suggest a

structured list of floral materials, which serves as a professional reference for the 
administrator. 
Inventory Tracking: Monitors real-time stock levels and flags shortages relative to 
confirmed bookings. The system implements a tiered alert mechanism, performing 
weekly inventory checks starting four weeks prior to an event and escalating to daily 
alerts during the final week. To address stock unavailability, the system includes a 
Seasonal Substitution feature that recommends alternative floral materials when 
requested items are out of stock or out of season, minimizing operational disruptions 
and unplanned procurement costs.  
Post-Event Return Tracking: Tracks the recovery of non-perishable assets (vases, 
stands, lighting), flags missing or damaged items, and updates inventory records 
accordingly. 
Price Validity and Reconfirmation: Manages the volatility of floral pricing by 
marking initial quotes as "tentative" and triggering automatic reconfirmation 
notifications for long-term bookings. 
Reports and Audit Logs: Generates timestamped logs and dashboard analytics 
regarding material usage, booking history, and business performance.

1.7 Definition of Terms 
The following terms are defined operationally as they are used and interpreted 
within the context of this study and the operations of Raflora Enterprises: 
Event Booking: The process of receiving, scheduling, and confirming client event 
reservations through the system, matched against available inventory and the event 
calendar. 
Flower Arrangement: refers to the structured composition of floral and decorative 
materials prepared for a specific client event, serving as the primary service output 
that the system's booking, inventory, and AI analysis features are designed to support. 
Gemini AI-Assisted Image Analysis: The integration of the Gemini AI API into the 
system to process client-uploaded inspiration images and translate visual data into a 
structured list of suggested floral materials. 
Inspiration Image: refers to a client-provided photo showing desired event themes, 
color schemes, or arrangement styles, uploaded through the system for AI-assisted 
analysis. 
Inventory Management: refers to the process of recording, tracking, and monitoring 
floral materials and supplies specific to Raflora Enterprises confirmed and upcoming 
event bookings, including stock level monitoring against booking requirements and 
the recording of material release. Post-Event Return Tracking: The mechanism used to monitor the return of 
non-perishable assets (such as vases, stands, and lighting equipment) following an 
event and flagging missing or damaged property for administrative follow-up.

REVIEW OF RELATED LITERATURE AND STUDIES 
 
2.1 Introduction 
This chapter presents a comprehensive review of related literature and studies 
essential to the architectural and functional design of the Flower Arrangement Event 
Booking and Inventory Management System with Gemini AI-Assisted Image 
Analysis. Unlike traditional retail systems that manage non-perishable hardware, the 
floral industry faces a unique dual challenge: the high perishability of inventory and 
the extreme visual complexity of client requirements. This review establishes the 
technical necessity for a system that can bridge the gap between unstructured visual 
inspiration and precise material quantification. 
The literature surveyed from both local and foreign sources explores the evolution of 
perishable inventory management, the logistics of event booking coordination, and 
the emerging role of AI-assisted tools for Small and Medium Enterprises (SMEs). 
Following this, a review of related studies evaluates existing web-based platforms for 
event and reservation management, highlighting their strengths and their failure to 
address the translation gap between reference images and stock deduction. By 
synthesizing these findings, this chapter establishes the Research Gap that the 
proposed system seeks to fill and presents the Conceptual Framework using the 
Input-Process-Output (IPO) model to illustrate the system’s operational logic within 
the specific context of Raflora Enterprises.

3.5 Project Evaluation 
The evaluation assessed usability and operational effectiveness during the RAD 
Cutover phase. Two approaches were used with Raflora staff: a survey questionnaire 
adapted from the ISO/IEC 25010 Software Quality Model evaluating the system 
across six quality characteristics: Functional Suitability, Usability, Reliability, 
Performance Efficiency, Security, and Compatibility; and a before-after performance 
comparison against the Section 1.1 baseline metrics of booking processing time, 
scheduling conflicts, and inventory shortage incidence.  
Respondents of the Study 
The respondents of the study consist of five (5) individuals, comprising the 
owner, the lead floral designer, and three administrative staff members of Raflora 
Enterprises. While this represents a small numerical sample, the researchers utilized 
a total population sampling technique. Since these five individuals constitute 100% of 
the administrative and decision-making workforce responsible for the booking and 
inventory management processes, they are the only qualified subjects capable of 
evaluating the system's impact on these specific internal workflows. While the 
business employs additional general staff for event setup, the selected respondents 
represent the total population of the system's intended end-users. Consequently, their 
feedback will provide a comprehensive and highly valid assessment of the system's 
usability and effectiveness within its intended organizational context.

 The evaluation of the system is divided into three parts to test its usability,
